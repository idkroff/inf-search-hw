Ancient Rome

Rome was one of the most powerful civilizations in the ancient world. Founded,
according to legend, by Romulus and Remus in 753 BCE, Rome grew from a small city-state
on the banks of the Tiber River into a vast empire spanning three continents.

The Roman Republic was governed by the Senate and two elected consuls. Julius Caesar's
rise to power and subsequent assassination in 44 BCE marked the transition to the
Roman Empire. Augustus became the first Roman emperor in 27 BCE.

Romans made lasting contributions to law, engineering, architecture, and language.
The Latin alphabet is the basis for many modern writing systems. Roman roads, aqueducts,
and the Colosseum still stand today as testaments to Roman engineering. Christianity
spread throughout the empire during the first centuries CE and eventually became the
official state religion under Emperor Constantine.
